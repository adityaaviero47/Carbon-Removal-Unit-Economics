# Executive memo: where the margin is in Indian carbon removal

**To:** Leadership (illustrative memo for a portfolio project)  **From:** Aditya Singh  **Date:** Nov 2026
**Basis:** model in `model/Carbon_Removal_Unit_Economics.xlsx`, base case unless stated. All figures are model outputs on public prices and stated assumptions, not results from a real project.

## Bottom line
1. **Biochar is a cost business, not a carbon-yield business.** At a USD 150 credit price against the USD 143 global supplier breakeven, base-case margin is **USD 7 per tonne (4.7%)**. Margin is positive in **69%** of 20,000 Monte Carlo draws. Delivery cost and price each swing margin far more than stalk supply, yield or carbon content (tornado chart). The operating lever is cost per tonne: feedstock logistics and reactor utilisation.
2. **Regen-ag soil carbon is an MRV-cost business.** A farmer earns about **INR 817 per acre per year** at a 50% revenue share (P10 to P90: INR 566 to 1,722). The developer keeps about **USD 3.7 per acre**, and MRV at USD 5 per acre takes **58%** of the developer's share. Scale per field agent and cheaper digital MRV decide viability.
3. **India's registration delay is a hidden cost.** Verra AFOLU projects in India take a median 796 days to register vs 514 days in the rest of Asia (CEEW). At a 12% discount rate that cuts the value of each credit by **8%**, and by **28%** for the slowest quarter of projects (1,689 vs 623 days). Cutting time to registration is worth as much as a price increase of the same size.
4. **ERW shows the highest margin per acre (USD 117), but on the thinnest evidence.** Measured field removal has come in well below claims (CarbonPlan on Lithos), so treat it as an option to test, not a base plan.
5. **Agroforestry pays farmers most per acre (about INR 2,565 a year)** but its margin here is before planting costs, which are not modelled.

## A check on our own lab result
My BITS pot trial found 2% biochar (w/w) gave the tallest tomato plants. In the field that dose is about **42 t of biochar per hectare**, which is roughly **99 years** of the biochar one hectare's own cotton stalks can make. Field programmes should test doses an order of magnitude lower (about 0.1 to 0.5%), and soil-benefit claims to farmers should not be based on pot-trial doses.

## Recommended next steps
- Build the biochar cost stack per tonne (stalk collection radius, transport, reactor uptime) and track it weekly against the USD 143 benchmark.
- For soil carbon, set a target MRV cost per acre and acres per field agent, and report both on the operations dashboard.
- Track days from project design to registration as a KPI for every AFOLU project.
- Re-run this model with Varaha's internal costs; only the Override column needs changing.

## Limits
Public, mostly global price and cost data; India-specific costs for ARR planting and soil practice change are not included; inputs are drawn independently in the Monte Carlo (real costs and prices may be correlated); no taxes, registry fees or financing structure; farmer share for ARR and soil is an assumption (Acorn's 80% is the high case).
